How does a rover know where it is on the Moon?

There is no GPS on the Moon. One way for a rover to find itself is to recognise a place it has seen before. That is hard on the lunar surface, because every crater field looks alike and the shadows move with the Sun. LunarSim is our open benchmark for this problem, and Galatea is our method for solving it.

These eight images show the same spot. The first is the reference “map” image. The other seven were taken from almost the same position, but with the Sun somewhere else. On the Moon there is no air to soften shadows, so moving the Sun changes almost everything the camera sees.

Test yourself against the rover’s AI

In Where on the Moon? you see what a rover’s camera sees and pick the matching place from six map images. The levels get harder as the Sun moves further from where it was when the map was made. After each round you see what our AI chose.

The rover AI picks the right place in 81–96% of these rounds, depending on the lighting. A strong AI trained on Earth photos manages only 52–84%. How do people compare? That is an open question, and one we hope the game can help answer.

What we found

35.8% → 78.8%
Share of rover views placed correctly on the first guess: best Earth-trained model vs. our full system (3,150 test views).
−21.5 points
What a model loses if it only ever sees one Sun direction during training. Varied lighting in training matters more than any architecture choice.
16% → 2.4%
Share of views that were right and became wrong after re-ranking: local matches used alone vs. fused with the global score. How evidence is combined matters more than the matcher.
